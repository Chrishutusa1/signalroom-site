Your Staff Already Use AI. Who Owns the Risk?

with Aaron Bach · Co-Founder and CTO, Liminal

Aaron Bach, co-founder and CTO of Liminal, joins Chris Hutchins to look at the controls, responsibilities and workforce habits that decide how AI is used in healthcare. The conversation runs from shadow AI and the cost of waiting for perfect controls to model choice, human judgment and who answers for a harmful outcome. Aaron closes with two practical tests: watch how an existing AI touchpoint responds to a behavioral control, and ask whose name would be on the incident report if an AI-assisted decision harmed a patient.

Show Notes

Aaron Bach, co-founder and CTO of Liminal, joins Chris Hutchins to look at the controls, responsibilities and workforce habits that decide how AI is used in healthcare. The conversation runs from shadow AI and the cost of waiting for perfect controls to model choice, human judgment and who answers for a harmful outcome. Aaron closes with two practical tests: watch how an existing AI touchpoint responds to a behavioral control, and ask whose name would be on the incident report if an AI-assisted decision harmed a patient.

What We Cover

  • Why waiting for perfect AI controls means never shipping, and why staff are already using AI
  • Aaron's path from software and regulated industries to co-founding Liminal
  • Research on shadow AI: employees using tools their organizations have not approved
  • Governing behavior on both sides, and why blocking and shaming backfire
  • What shipping the governance you have looks like: one team, one real use case
  • Why useful AI use cases come from frontline staff rather than generic healthcare lists
  • Governance as an ongoing manage function, and committees that become theater
  • Multi-model strategy, and why no single platform covers everything called AI
  • Prompt training, hallucinations, and keeping people in command of the output
  • Where risk sits: patient communication, observability, and compliance
  • Two moves for health system leaders this quarter

Key Takeaways

  • Your staff are already using AI. Per Bach, waiting for processes, committee decisions and documentation to be perfect means leaders probably never get there, and employees keep using the tools in the meantime. He argues for starting with one team on a real use case under the controls already in place.
  • Governance is a running management job. Bach places AI governance in the manage function of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework: ongoing action rather than a policy on a shelf. He is wary of committees that are representative on paper but never decide anything.
  • People carry the risk, not the platform. No single platform covers every capability, and a person's ability to judge whether AI output is correct has not been replaced. His test for leaders: if an AI-assisted decision harmed a patient tomorrow, whose name is on the incident report, and which control should have caught it?

Examples Used in the Conversation

  • Research Liminal paid for in mid-2023 in which employees across industries described how they actually used AI at work
  • The CISO as Hollywood special effects: nobody notices when the job is done well, and everybody notices when it is not
  • Agile software development as a parallel for why long up-front planning stops working when the environment changes quickly
  • Watching an AI agent used for software development work like an enthusiastic junior employee who needs to be stopped before going down the wrong path
  • A wrong AI-generated message sent through a patient portal without review
  • Chris's football offensive-line analogy for shared accountability across departments

These examples reflect Aaron Bach's perspective and experience, offered as leadership insight rather than legal, clinical or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Aaron Bach

Aaron Bach is the co-founder and CTO of Liminal, which guides enterprises in the secure adoption and management of generative AI through scalable infrastructure, data security and multi-model safeguards. He has more than 15 years of experience across software, hardware, AI, IoT and enterprise architecture, and has led engineering teams delivering large-scale technology for Fortune 500 companies. Before co-founding Liminal he was CTO of a pharmacy savings platform and head of engineering for innovation at FIS, where he worked on technical strategy, architecture and venture incubation. His bio also notes that he is a top-10 contributor to the open-source project Home Assistant. Connect with him on LinkedIn.
