AI Governance From Theory to Operational Reality | Bennett Borden

with Bennett Borden

From AI governance theory to operational reality. Bennett Borden — former CIA intelligence analyst, AI lawyer, and founder of Clarion AI Partners — on what governance looks like when it leaves the page: risk, trust, defensibility, and the human judgment that scaling automation must preserve.

Show Notes

A significant portion of repetitive legal work can now be performed more effectively through AI-assisted workflows — but the interesting question is not the technology, it is what that shift means for the professionals, firms, and organizations navigating it. Bennett Borden, founder and CEO of Clarion AI Partners, came to that question by an unusual route: from intelligence analysis at the CIA, to practicing law, to building an AI-native law and technology practice. He joins Chris Hutchins to move AI governance from theory to operational reality.

What We Cover

  • An unusual career path — from CIA intelligence analysis to law, how analytics shaped his approach, and why he chose to build rather than simply advise
  • What AI governance actually means — governance on paper vs in practice, lessons from work involving NIST and the EU AI Act, and what boards and executive teams should really be asking
  • Risk, trust, and defensibility — where AI reduces risk vs creates new exposure, preserving human judgment while scaling automation, and building systems that stay explainable, measurable, and defensible
  • Legal work in the age of AI — contract review and document analysis, where AI creates measurable value today, what remains uniquely human, and how the attorney role is changing
  • Public interest and human impact — child-safety and suicide-prevention initiatives, the Utah Office of AI Policy and its mitigation-sandbox work, and responsible deployment in high-consequence environments

Key Takeaways

  • The future belongs to neither the AI maximalists nor the avoiders. It belongs to those who can tell where automation creates value, where judgment remains essential, and how trust is built between the two.
  • Governance on paper is not governance. The test is whether an AI program stays explainable, measurable, and defensible in practice — not whether principles were published.
  • Defensibility is the bridge from experimentation to adoption — systems whose decisions can be evidenced, measured, and stood behind after the fact.

Frameworks & Initiatives Mentioned

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework
  • EU AI Act
  • Utah Office of AI Policy — AI mitigation sandbox
  • Governance-on-paper vs governance-in-practice; explainable / measurable / defensible AI systems

About Bennett Borden

Bennett Borden is the Founder and CEO of Clarion AI Partners, an integrated law, technology, and strategy firm helping organizations deliver AI the right way. A former Big Law partner and Chief Data Scientist whose path runs from intelligence analysis to the practice of law, he works with executives and AI leaders to move from experimentation to production with systems that are practical, scalable, and legally durable.
