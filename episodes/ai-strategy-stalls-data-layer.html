AI Strategy Stalls at the Data Layer: Privacy-Preserving AI & Governance for Healthcare

with Sid Dutta, Founder & CEO, PrivateClave AI

Sid Dutta joins the show to explain why most healthcare AI strategies fail before a single model is deployed — and why the answer isn't better algorithms, it's runtime AI governance, privacy-preserving AI, and a data layer that can finally travel with its own controls.

Show Notes

Most healthcare AI initiatives never make it past pilot — and the reason isn't the model. It's the data layer. In this episode of The Signal Room, Chris Hutchins sits down with Sid Dutta, Founder and CEO of PrivateClave AI and a cybersecurity veteran who led data protection across American Express, Worldpay, Activision Blizzard King, and Micro Focus / OpenText, to unpack why AI strategy for healthcare breaks down before a single inference runs, and what runtime governance and privacy-preserving AI can actually unlock once fundamentals hold.

What We Cover

  • Why healthcare AI strategy stalls at the data layer, not the model layer
  • The shift from institutional-centric to network-centric healthcare data
  • Privacy-preserving AI, runtime guardrails, and intent-based access control
  • Shadow AI, agentic AI (MCP clients and servers), and the new risk surface
  • Why data access and data protection must stop being separate problems
  • What the next phase of healthcare AI looks like once fundamentals hold

Key Takeaways

  • The data layer is where AI strategy wins or stalls. Native EHR controls don't travel with the data once it leaves Epic or Cerner. The moment PHI moves to a co-pilot, MCP server, or third-party analytics platform, native protections are gone.
  • Stop treating access and protection as separate problems. Most organizations have one team trying to enable innovation and another trying to block risk — the friction creates shadow AI, which creates more risk than either side acknowledges.
  • The real differentiator isn't which model you chose. It's how confidently you can use your data with that model. Confidence comes from visibility, runtime control, and policy enforcement at the data layer.
  • Readiness red flags are cultural, not technical. "Block-first" mentalities, role-based access in a non-deterministic AI world, and shared data marts where nobody owns the risk are the real signals an organization isn't ready to collaborate safely.

Frameworks & Tools Mentioned

  • Tokenization, format-preserving encryption, and deterministic encryption
  • Data de-identification and desensitization at runtime
  • Homomorphic encryption, federated learning, and differential privacy
  • Secure enclaves / trusted execution environments / confidential computing
  • Data clean rooms and secure multi-party collaboration environments
  • Model Context Protocol (MCP) clients and servers in healthcare agentic AI workflows
  • Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) and its blind spots
  • Intent-based and context-aware dynamic access control

About Sid Dutta

Sid Dutta is the Founder and CEO of PrivateClave AI, solving the deep-rooted challenges of protecting sensitive data in the age of AI. A cybersecurity and data protection veteran, he has held senior data protection roles at American Express, Worldpay, and Activision Blizzard King, and served as Product Head of Voltage Data Security at Micro Focus (now OpenText). His work centers on runtime visibility, AI governance, and privacy-by-default protections that make it possible to analyze and collaborate on sensitive healthcare, financial services, and enterprise data across legacy systems and AI workflows — without exposing it.
