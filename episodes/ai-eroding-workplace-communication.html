Show Notes

Are your employees using AI as a tool, or is it quietly eroding your company’s culture? In this episode of The Signal Room, Chris Hutchins talks with Katherine Tuominen — organizational narrative strategist and founder of Catalyst Brand Strategy — about why digital transformation is primarily a trust event, not a technical one, and about the growing friction between top-down executive directives and the lived operational realities of frontline workers.

What We Cover

  • Why digital transformation is primarily a trust event, not a technical one
  • What “narrative debt” is — and what happens when public values stop matching workplace reality
  • How “AI slop” (the sudden “delve” epidemic) erodes authentic communication
  • Why automated AI email threads fail to create real team alignment
  • The compliance trap: how frontline workers accidentally leak private data into public LLMs
  • The promotion paradox: why forcing top performers into management destroys trust

Key Takeaways

  • Transformation is a trust event. Adoption succeeds or fails on whether people trust leadership, not on the sophistication of the tool.
  • Narrative debt compounds quietly. When stated values stop matching how work actually feels, the gap accrues like debt — and eventually comes due.
  • “AI slop” flattens authentic voice. Outsourcing communication to AI produces generic corporate language (the “delve” epidemic) that signals disengagement rather than alignment.
  • Automated email threads don’t build alignment. Real coordination comes from human exchange, not AI round-trips.
  • Public LLMs are a live compliance risk. Frontline staff pasting confidential data — including patient details — into public models can expose protected information without realizing it.
  • The promotion paradox erodes trust. Forcing your best technical contributors into management roles they don’t want destroys both performance and trust.

About Katherine Tuominen

Katherine Tuominen is an organizational narrative strategist, marketing strategist, and publicist, and the founder of Catalyst Brand Strategy. She helps companies treat digital transformation as a trust event rather than a purely technical one, focusing on the friction between top-down executive directives and the lived realities of frontline workers. Her work centers on “narrative debt” — the cultural cost that accrues when an organization’s stated values stop matching how work actually feels — and on helping leaders build psychological safety, resist “AI slop” in corporate communication, and stand up safe AI communications infrastructure that protects confidential data.
