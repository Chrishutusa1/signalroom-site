Your Practice Needs a Clearer View of Its Money

with Tomer Lankri and Erez Lankri · Co-Founders, Lucid Financials

Erez Lankri, co-founder and CEO, and Tomer Lankri, co-founder and CTO of Lucid Financials, join Chris Hutchins to talk about financial visibility, grounded AI and accountable support for medical practices. Delayed books and scattered information shape the decisions practice owners make every day. Tomer and Erez describe how an AI answer can be traced back to its source record, and why a named professional stays responsible as more of the financial work is automated.

Show Notes

Erez Lankri, co-founder and CEO, and Tomer Lankri, co-founder and CTO of Lucid Financials, join Chris Hutchins to talk about financial visibility, grounded AI and accountable support for medical practices. Delayed books and scattered information shape the decisions practice owners make every day. Tomer and Erez describe how an AI answer can be traced back to its source record, and why a named professional stays responsible as more of the financial work is automated.

What We Cover

  • How two brothers who grew up around their parents' CPA office ended up building AI-supported finance services for medical practices
  • Four blind spots: doctors trained to treat patients rather than run a business, money scattered across processors, vendors, payroll and entities, books that arrive months late, and decisions made on gut feeling
  • Connecting patient-management data to the books so cash flow and payroll can be forecast ahead of time
  • A practice sale that stalled over a liability of about two million dollars between entities
  • Keeping a human in the loop, and why regulated work such as taxes still needs a responsible professional
  • Rule-based categorization and AI groundwork that leave the CPA free for strategy
  • Multi-entity reporting and fresh forecasts for practices that already have a bookkeeper or CFO
  • Grounding: an AI answer that cannot be given without its justification, down to the invoice
  • Measuring AI against human error in traditional bookkeeping
  • A missed tax credit above $50,000, found within a few weeks
  • Why the data architecture matters more than the model

Key Takeaways

  • Ground the answer in the record. Tomer says an AI used for finance should not be able to reply without a justification tied to the underlying data, so that a number can always be checked against the invoice behind it.
  • A named person stays accountable. Erez says a practice should always have someone who owns the results. At Lucid, AI handles groundwork such as rule-based categorization, and a professional reviews each step of the process.
  • Fix the data before the model. Tomer argues the biggest step forward for any business is a data architecture the models can work with. When patient-management data is connected to the books, an owner can see a payroll problem coming before payday.

Examples Tomer and Erez Used

  • A practice owner set on selling this year, whose books showed a liability of about two million dollars between entities
  • A client who moved from a long-time accountant to a healthcare specialist and had a tax credit above $50,000 identified within a couple of weeks
  • A forecast that goes stale the morning after it is finished, and how managed books keep the data fresh
  • New clients arriving with books they would rate two out of ten

These examples are the guests' own accounts of their work, offered as business insight rather than financial, tax or legal advice.
