Show Notes

Most healthcare AI is sold on a demo and a promise. Dr. AJ Blood did the opposite: he put his own AI tool through the same evidence bar we hold medicine to and published the results in the New England Journal of Medicine and JAMA. A practicing cardiologist and intensivist at Brigham and Women's Hospital and the co-founder and CEO of AIwithCare, he explains how RECTIFIER reads structured and unstructured chart data to find patients eligible for clinical trials, why every decision stays human-in-the-loop, and why workflow, not the model, decides whether these tools succeed.

What We Cover

  • Why it can take decades to move a discovery from the bench to the bedside, and where trials bottleneck
  • The screening problem: roughly 90% of charts opened are for patients who turn out ineligible
  • What RECTIFIER does: reads structured and unstructured EHR data against inclusion and exclusion criteria, and cites its evidence
  • The evidence bar: a New England Journal of Medicine accuracy study and a JAMA randomized trial
  • The headline result: an 84% increase in trial enrollment (p=0.04)
  • Human-in-the-loop by design: the AI surfaces and sources, the clinician confirms
  • Other use cases across the health system: pediatric GI referrals, ALS trial screening, registries
  • Watching for bias: monitoring across age, sex, race, ethnicity, and language, live and across sites
  • Why “it is not the model, it is the workflow,” and how to avoid death-by-pilot

Key Takeaways

  • Hold AI to the same evidence bar as medicine. Blood published a non-inferiority accuracy study in the New England Journal of Medicine and a randomized trial in JAMA showing RECTIFIER-assisted screening safely and accurately accelerated enrollment, an 84% increase (p=0.04).
  • Human-in-the-loop, always. The model never makes a medical or eligibility decision autonomously; it surfaces and cites relevant chart evidence so trained staff confirm or deny far faster, turning a 90%-ineligible slog into efficient screening.
  • It is the workflow, not the model. Echoing “it is not the model, it is the harness,” Blood argues adoption fails when tools add steps; success comes from narrow changes that fit how clinical and research teams already work.

Examples AJ Used

  • Roughly 90% of charts opened are for patients who end up ineligible, the biggest bucket of a principal investigator's cost
  • A pediatric GI clinic using a customized RECTIFIER to triage community referrals and get high-risk kids seen sooner
  • A neurology group screening every clinic patient for ALS trials, where time to enrollment is critical
  • The patient's half-day journey (drive, park, wait, then 5 to 15 minutes with the clinician) that remote, asynchronous care redesigns

These results and examples reflect Dr. Blood's published work and account; RECTIFIER keeps a clinician in the loop for every decision and does not provide medical advice.
