AI in Emergency Medicine: Does AI Make a Better Doctor?

with Dr. Harvey Castro · Emergency Physician & Healthcare AI Speaker

Harvey Castro, MD, MBA, is a board-certified emergency physician in Dallas, a healthcare AI speaker, and the creator of DR GPT. He joins Chris Hutchins on where AI belongs in a hospital: making care instructions easier for patients to understand, moving medicine from reactive to proactive, and the questions leaders should ask about trust, culture and financial return before adopting an AI tool.

Show Notes

Harvey Castro, MD, MBA, is a board-certified emergency physician in Dallas, a healthcare AI speaker, and the creator of DR GPT. He joins Chris Hutchins on where AI belongs in a hospital: making care instructions easier for patients to understand, moving medicine from reactive to proactive, and the questions leaders should ask about trust, culture and financial return before adopting an AI tool.

What We Cover

Harvey traces his path into emergency medicine to a 12-hour wait in a New York emergency room as a 12-year-old, and follows it through early medical apps, building his own emergency rooms, and writing one of the first books on ChatGPT in healthcare within minutes of its release.

Chris and Harvey then get practical: how generative AI can make discharge instructions understandable for children, teenagers and patients from other cultures, why the value of human plus AI depends on the human, what proactive care could look like, and the three pillars leaders should weigh before deploying AI. They close on what Harvey would tell medical students and young people choosing their careers now.

Key Takeaways

Communication is part of the care. With patient identifiers removed, Harvey used a language model to turn pediatric discharge instructions into coloring pages, comic strips for teenagers, and translations that account for culture as well as language.

Human plus AI depends on the human. Whether a doctor gets better with AI depends on how well that doctor understands the tools, their limits, and how creatively they combine them with medicine.

Reactive medicine waits too long. Harvey contrasts his father, who died of a heart attack with no warning, with his mother, whose early cancer diagnosis let her get treatment. He describes his own offline digital twin of wearable, record, genetic and lab data, with an AI agent that reviews new research and recommendations he checks himself for hallucinations.

Three pillars before adoption. Harvey weighs financial ROI, culture and trust, and says your AI will only move as fast as the trust. A quick culture check: ask each executive what they thought of AI four years ago and what they do differently today.

Right size the model. Harvey asks whether every use case needs the most expensive model, or whether something smaller built in house fits the need and the budget as token and energy costs climb.

Examples in this episode reflect Dr. Castro's personal experience and observations and are offered as leadership insight. They are not validated outcomes and are not clinical, legal or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Dr. Harvey Castro

Harvey Castro, MD, MBA, is a board-certified emergency physician, healthcare AI speaker, and advisor to the CEO of Phantom Space. Known as DR GPT, he is a former CEO of Trusted Medical Centers, a five-time TEDx speaker, and the author of more than 45 books, including AI in Emergency Medicine (Wiley).
