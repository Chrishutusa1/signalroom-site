AI in Hospitals: Building the AI-Native Hospital

with David Niewolny · Director of Business Development, Healthcare and Medical, NVIDIA

AI in hospitals is shifting from separate tools toward a coordinated layer that supports everyone delivering care, and David Niewolny of NVIDIA explains what it takes to build an AI-native hospital. David, director of business development for healthcare and medical at NVIDIA, joins Chris Hutchins to talk through clinical documentation, surgical robotics, explainability and digital twins in healthcare. His advice for hospital leaders is direct: find the single biggest problem in the organization before choosing an AI project.

Show Notes

AI in hospitals is shifting from separate tools toward a coordinated layer that supports everyone delivering care, and David Niewolny of NVIDIA explains what it takes to build an AI-native hospital. David, director of business development for healthcare and medical at NVIDIA, joins Chris Hutchins to talk through clinical documentation, surgical robotics, explainability and digital twins in healthcare. His advice for hospital leaders is direct: find the single biggest problem in the organization before choosing an AI project.

What We Cover

  • What makes a hospital AI-native, and why so much of the data a hospital creates still waits on people to act on it
  • How AI moved from perception to generative, agentic and physical AI
  • An X-ray room that guides patient positioning and checks image quality before the patient leaves
  • Why David cannot name a clinical role AI has eliminated, and the goal of seeing far more patients
  • Preoperative surgical planning, rehearsal in simulation and the kinematics data behind robotic surgery
  • Open models, explainability and the regulator's job of confirming AI is safe and effective
  • Ambient clinical documentation and giving time back to the patient encounter
  • Surgical autonomy compared with driver assistance, and the hospital as an orchestration layer
  • Conversational agents for questions before and after a procedure
  • Synthetic data, simulation testing and the burden of proving outcomes
  • Digital twins in healthcare for designing a hospital and its workflows before construction
  • Where a hospital executive should start in the next twelve months

Key Takeaways

  • An AI-native hospital puts its own intelligence to work. Per Niewolny, hospitals have digitized patient and operational data, yet people still act on most of it by hand, and digitization added workloads that have little to do with patient care. Agentic and physical AI working as one coordinated layer is how he sees that time going back to clinicians, administrators and patients.
  • Augmentation, with humans in control. Niewolny says he cannot name a role AI has made disappear, pointing to radiology, where efficiency created demand for more imaging and more radiologists. He compares surgical robotics to driver assistance with warnings and automatic braking, and does not expect fully self-driving surgery.
  • Start with the biggest problem. His advice to hospital leaders is to skip pilots that cannot deliver real impact and aim AI at the single biggest problem the organization faces. Hospitals are buyers of technology, so the medtech and software partners they choose shape the result.

Examples Used in the Conversation

  • An X-ray system that guides positioning, sets itself up and checks image quality before the patient steps out of the room
  • A study David saw at a surgical robotics conference, by Andrew Hung, linking how surgeons move their hands to patient outcomes
  • NVIDIA's work with Abridge on foundation models trained on clinical and medical language
  • A Hippocratic AI digital agent that answers questions about post-procedure care
  • NVIDIA's work with BMW to simulate an entire manufacturing facility before construction
  • Hospital supply rooms that remain far more manual than an Amazon warehouse
  • Chris's first look at a gamma knife, and real-time coaching for care teams he heard about from researchers at Stanford

These examples reflect David Niewolny's perspective and experience and are offered as leadership insight. Technical examples and forecasts are the speakers' views, not validated clinical outcomes, and they are not legal, clinical or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About David Niewolny

David Niewolny is the director of business development for healthcare and medical at NVIDIA, where he focuses on easing the adoption of AI in healthcare and medical applications. He has 18 years of experience building and leading healthcare-focused businesses at AWS, Real-Time Innovations, NXP and Freescale Semiconductor, with a long record of work on wireless, cloud, IoT and AI in healthcare and medical applications. He holds an MBA from the University of Texas at Austin and a B.S. in biomedical and biochemical engineering from Iowa State University. Connect with him on LinkedIn.
