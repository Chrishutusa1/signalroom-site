Cell and Gene Therapy: Why a Cure Isn't Enough

with Andy Moye · Co-Founder & CEO, Glidepath Health

Show Notes

Cell and gene therapies can cure disease, but a cure only matters if the patient can actually get it. Andy Moye, PhD, co-founder and CEO of Glidepath Health, joins Chris Hutchins on the operational gap between an advanced therapy and the patient who needs it: scheduling, travel, reimbursement, and fragmented data. A former US Naval Flight Officer and author of From Bunker Hill to the Boardroom, Andy also unpacks the leadership habits behind empowered teams, clear intent, and better decisions under pressure.

What We Cover

Andy traces his path from the US Navy into cancer diagnostics and advanced therapies, including digital pathology at Paige, and explains what changed in oncology over the last 25 years. The conversation then turns to cell and gene therapies, how CRISPR based treatments can effectively cure diseases like sickle cell, and why most eligible patients still never receive them.

Chris and Andy work through the three barriers between a working therapy and the patient: cost, geography, and a year-long manual scheduling cascade. Andy describes what Glidepath is building to orchestrate that delivery, where AI belongs in prior authorization and reimbursement, the fragmented data problem underneath it all, and the leadership habits he carried from the military into a startup.

Key Takeaways

The science works; the delivery fails patients. Andy says 80 to 90 percent of patients eligible for CAR T therapy do not get it, lost to cost, distance from academic centers, and a scheduling process that can run more than a year on spreadsheets.

The bottleneck has moved. In 2020 one cell and gene therapy was approved; today there are 48, with over 300 late-stage US trials. The health system is not built to manage that volume, and Glidepath builds an AI orchestration layer that turns each therapy protocol into automated tasks.

AI asymmetry in reimbursement. Insurers already use AI to screen prior authorizations at scale, while hospitals mostly do not, which Andy calls bringing a knife to a gunfight. He sees AI generating the clinical letter of necessity and chasing status so patients start therapy sooner.

Fragmented data is the ground truth problem. Records still do not follow the patient across systems, and Andy aims to put all the data for one therapy in one place and make it visible to research, pharma, and the patient.

Judge decisions, not outcomes. Drawing on his book and military after-action reviews, Andy argues leaders should examine the judgment behind a result, not only the result, to build better decision making over time.

Figures and examples in this episode reflect Andy Moye experience and Glidepath Health observations and are offered as leadership insight. They are not validated outcomes and are not legal, clinical, billing or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Andy Moye

Andy Moye, PhD, is the co-founder and CEO of Glidepath Health, which builds AI to orchestrate the delivery of cell and gene therapies. A former US Naval Flight Officer, he spent more than two decades in cancer diagnostics and advanced therapies, including digital pathology at Paige, and holds a PhD in health economics. He is the author of From Bunker Hill to the Boardroom: Battle-Tested Leadership for Today Executives. Connect with him on LinkedIn.
