Show Notes

AI security risks in healthcare demand attention before deployment, not after. Anitha Mareedu, a network security engineer with experience spanning advertising, social media, security vendors, and chip design at Cadence, argues that deploying AI without securing it is deploying a vulnerability. The conversation, recorded at the Put Data First Conference, maps what healthcare leaders must understand about the threat landscape that arrives with every AI deployment.

What We Cover

  • Why the CIA triad of confidentiality, integrity, and availability is prioritized differently across industries, and what that means for healthcare systems operating under HIPAA, NIST, and SOC frameworks.
  • How agentic AI creates new security challenges around access control, deployment boundaries, and the blast radius of autonomous decisions.
  • Where the cybersecurity talent pipeline is breaking down and why the pace of AI advancement has outrun the pace of workforce readiness.
  • What layer-one through layer-seven security looks like when an AI model is part of the stack, and why traditional perimeter thinking misses the new attack surfaces.
  • How healthcare leaders should frame risk conversations at the board level, particularly around national security dimensions that most private organizations never see.

Key Takeaways

AI security is not a feature added after deployment. It is a prerequisite. Every AI system introduced into healthcare expands the attack surface in ways traditional frameworks were not designed to address.

Different industries prioritize the CIA triad differently. Government agencies prioritize confidentiality. B2B organizations prioritize availability. Healthcare must protect personally identifiable information while maintaining uptime, which creates tradeoffs that security teams must decide explicitly.

Continuous self-directed learning is now the only way to keep pace. Technology now changes every year rather than every 5 or 10, and cybersecurity professionals who wait for formal training will fall behind the threat landscape.

Frameworks & Tools Mentioned

  • CIA triad (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability)
  • HIPAA, NIST, SOC compliance frameworks
  • Firewalls: Cisco, Palo Alto Networks, Check Point
  • IDPS tools, endpoint security, user identification systems
  • Cadence (semiconductor design)
