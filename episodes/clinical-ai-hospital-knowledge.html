Can You Trust Clinical AI When No One Is Asking the Right Questions?

with Dr. Declan Kelly · Founder and CEO, Eolas Medical

Dr. Declan Kelly, emergency medicine physician, software engineer and Founder and CEO of Eolas Medical, joins Chris Hutchins to look at the layer clinical AI keeps skipping: the hospital's own governed knowledge. They cover why external evidence only gets a clinician part of the way, how context turns answer engines into systems that can act, why probabilistic AI has to sit inside a clinical safety system, and what changes as Eolas scales from the NHS into US health systems.

Show Notes

Dr. Declan Kelly, emergency medicine physician, software engineer and Founder and CEO of Eolas Medical, joins Chris Hutchins to look at the layer clinical AI keeps skipping: the hospital's own governed knowledge. They cover why external evidence only gets a clinician part of the way, how context turns answer engines into systems that can act, why probabilistic AI has to sit inside a clinical safety system, and what changes as Eolas scales from the NHS into US health systems.

What We Cover

  • Declan's path from emergency medicine to building Eolas Medical
  • Why local knowledge such as formularies, policies and referral pathways is the last mile of clinical decisions
  • Data silos, ambient listeners, and AI as the connector across a health system
  • Moving from a disease-centric model toward prevention and longitudinal data
  • Going from a physician's training to never be wrong to building a company
  • General clinical models compared with what a model must know about one hospital
  • The troponin test analogy for building safety systems around probabilistic AI
  • Retrieval-augmented generation and fine-tuning for hospital knowledge
  • Context as the difference between answer engines and agentic systems
  • Managing and accessing knowledge, and capturing tacit knowledge at scale
  • Regulation, deflationary economics and clinical safety review
  • Transparency and source verification while regulation remains unsettled
  • Problem-focused adoption, and when a solution is strong enough to change the list
  • Lessons from scaling across the NHS that change in US health systems

Key Takeaways

  • The missing piece was never the model. Per Kelly, external knowledge such as national guidelines and evidence platforms might get a clinician 50% of the way. The last mile, such as what is on this hospital's formulary, how to refer to cardiology or how to book a scan overnight, lives in local documents or in people's heads, and he estimates roughly 10% of that knowledge is codified.
  • Safety comes from the system around the model. Kelly compares clinical AI to the troponin blood test, which misses roughly 2% of heart attacks at three hours yet is extremely useful because of the checks and clinical judgment built around it. AI is probabilistic and will never be 100% accurate, so the work is in building that system. At Eolas, every question is reviewed by a separate answer engine for clinical safety.
  • Transparency stands in for missing regulation. With no regulator requiring years of trials before clinicians use AI tools, Kelly says the ability for a clinician to verify where an answer came from is vital. He adds that US clinicians need that knowledge surfaced inside the EHR and the ambient listener instead of a standalone app.

Examples Used in the Conversation

  • Finding local guidance in seconds for a patient having a major heart attack or a patient whose cancer treatment has failed
  • Ambient listeners capturing the full consultation when a doctor typically writes down about 10% of what was said
  • Eolas's own internal Claude instance connected to most of the company's data sources
  • OpenEvidence's rapid adoption among US physicians without a regulatory gate
  • Ambient listeners classed as Class I, and in some cases Class IIa, devices in Europe
  • Convolutional neural networks for stroke and fracture detection as a solution that changed hospital priorities
  • Hospitals across Northern Ireland moving from paper to an electronic record over the last three or four years
  • Chris's sticky-note test at the nurses' station, and a physician's warning that AI fails when no one talks to the nurses

These examples reflect Dr. Declan Kelly's perspective and experience and are offered as leadership insight. They are not legal, clinical or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Dr. Declan Kelly

Dr. Declan Kelly is the Founder and CEO of Eolas Medical, a clinical knowledge platform that uses AI to index a health system's own policies, guidelines and procedures so clinicians can get answers within seconds. He practiced as a physician for about five years, mostly in emergency medicine, and is also a software engineer who began building tools for his own hospital. Eolas, the Irish word for knowledge, launched about four years ago. Declan says it is now live in about 500 sites, including about 90% of acute NHS trusts and US organizations such as Stanford, Mass General and Boston Children's. Connect with him on LinkedIn.
