Show Notes

Patients now start with ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity, not a list of blue links, and AI is describing clinics before those clinics get a say. Darcy Sullivan, founder of Propel Marketing & Design and host of the Clinic Marketing Podcast, explains what actually makes a clinic visible when patients search AI-first: trust signals, reviews that name the services you treat, an accurate Google Business Profile, and content written for humans, not churned out by AI. She and Chris separate the hype from the work clinics really need to do.

What We Cover

  • Why patient search moved from keywords to conversational, AI-first questions, and what that changes for clinics
  • The layered approach: earning trust from patients and from Google and AI search engines at the same time
  • Trust signals that matter, including reviews that name specific services and conditions, not just five-star counts
  • Why your Google Business Profile and NAP (name, address, phone) consistency is not a social channel and needs its own care
  • What gets a clinic cited in an AI answer versus left out, and why thin three-sentence service pages lose
  • The line between responsible and permissible AI use, and keeping a human in the loop at every step
  • Lazy AI content: the tells (em dashes, over-bolding, formulaic copy) and why “garbage in, garbage out” applies
  • How to run a first AI-search visibility audit using ChatGPT or Grok, and reading your analytics
  • Where to stop spending (brand-name ads, dead social) and reinvest in SEO and AI search

Key Takeaways

  • AI describes your clinic before you do. Patients ask ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity conversational questions, so visibility now depends on trust signals, accurate listings, and detailed service content, not just ranking position.
  • Reviews and content have to name the work. Darcy says reviews and pages that specifically mention the services you offer and the conditions you treat are what earn citations; three-sentence service pages and generic AI copy don't compete.
  • Keep a human in the loop, and don't let “a kid” run AI. Use AI as an assistant for ideation and drafts, but fact-check and align everything to your brand before publishing, and hire experienced help rather than whoever is youngest.

Examples Darcy Used

  • The shift from typing “Facebook CEO” to asking “who is the CEO of Facebook” as voice and AI search changed behavior
  • A patient away for the summer asking AI for a clinic in a new area that matches how another clinic treats a condition
  • Rewording review requests from “will you leave us a review?” to “share what brought you in and how your experience was”
  • Clinics paying for brand-name ads that mostly capture returning visitors who would have booked anyway

These examples reflect Darcy's perspective and experience, not independently verified findings.
