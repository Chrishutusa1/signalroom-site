Clinical Denial Management: Where AI Meets Hospital Workflows

with Gretchen Heinen · Founder and CEO, Authsnap

Clinical denial management takes more than automation, and Gretchen Heinen, a registered nurse and the founder and CEO of Authsnap, explains why. Gretchen joins Chris Hutchins to walk through how payers communicate denials to hospitals, why clinical denials need human clinical review, how Authsnap keeps patient identifiers away from the language model, and what small and rural hospitals are up against. Her reason for building the company is plain: she watched patients go without care because of a sheet of paper.

What We Cover

  • Gretchen's path from microbiology to nursing to founder, and a career spent where insurance dollars meet patient care
  • Two lessons from the bedside: nothing in a founder's day is as urgent as CPR, and how to solve problems without every resource you need
  • The patient stories behind Authsnap, including a chemotherapy regimen denied because the patient was too sick for the third drug
  • What makes a clinical denial different from a billing error, and why policy interpretation makes it hard
  • Starting without an IT integration, and a pricing model that lets hospitals begin within 48 hours of a signed contract
  • The many ways payers communicate denials, from letters and clawbacks to spreadsheets, phone calls and remark codes
  • Why fixing denials starts with knowing who opens the mail
  • AI governance, de-identifying clinical notes and a nurse reviewing every case
  • Sharing denial patterns with clinical teams, such as the wrong patient status chosen for stroke patients on Tuesday nights
  • Trust, culture and listening to hospital leaders under pressure
  • What Authsnap measures, and the physician time lost to prior authorization and denials
  • Why rural hospitals are a focus, and what Gretchen expects in the next three years

Key Takeaways

  • Clinical denials are a workflow problem as much as a technology problem. Heinen says the biggest surprise after working behind the scenes of hundreds of hospitals was how differently payers communicate denials, and how many people in separate departments touch a single case. She argues that anyone offering an AI fix should first be able to explain how a denial letter reaches the right desk.
  • Privacy has to be engineered. Patient names appear throughout physician notes, so removing a header does not de-identify a record. Authsnap builds de-identification processes and barriers between its data and the language model, trains its software with its own clinicians, and has a human, at minimum a nurse, review every case before it goes out.
  • Small hospitals need tools built for them. Heinen says Authsnap has seen rural hospitals with $750,000 to $1.5 million a month affected by denials and no spare staff to write appeals. Larger vendors told her they are built for scale, so Authsnap built a solution that rural hospitals can use.

Examples Used in the Conversation

  • A cancer patient ordered a three-drug chemotherapy regimen who could tolerate only two of the drugs, which led to a denial and a delay in care
  • An Authsnap software engineer whose mother died after a denied claim, while her sister with the same cancer gene risk received preventive screening
  • A patient intubated in the trauma ICU after a car crash, with medical and auto insurers on separate systems
  • Chris's experience with explanation of benefits data where denials never reached the line item, and the paper EOB that proved the data existed
  • An emergency department pattern of the wrong patient status chosen for stroke patients on Tuesday nights
  • A Blue Cross Blue Shield memo noting more anemia diagnoses without more blood products ordered
  • Authsnap's online denial calculator

These examples reflect Gretchen Heinen's perspective and experience and are offered as leadership insight. Figures and forecasts are the speakers' views, not validated outcomes, and they are not legal, clinical, billing or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Gretchen Heinen

Gretchen Heinen, RN, PHN, BSN, is the founder and CEO of Authsnap, an AI-enabled healthcare technology company focused on reducing insurance denials, protecting revenue integrity and improving patient access to medically necessary care. A registered nurse with more than a decade of experience across trauma ICU, emergency medicine, utilization management and revenue cycle operations, she has seen firsthand how denials delay care, exhaust clinicians and financially harm patients and health systems. She built Authsnap as a human-in-the-loop platform that combines clinical expertise with responsible AI to help providers overturn inappropriate denials and prevent future ones.
