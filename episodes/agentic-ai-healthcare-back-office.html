AI Takes Work. What Comes Next?

with Michael Johns · Business Transformation Consultant, Lydonia Technologies

Michael Johns, business transformation consultant at Lydonia Technologies, joins Chris Hutchins to work through where agentic AI lands first in a health system: the back office. They cover which revenue cycle, claims and finance tasks are ready for autonomous agents, what should never be handed over, and how least privileged access and audit trails keep agent decisions defensible. Michael closes with what separates successful programs: treating automation as a program, telling people what is in it for them, and keeping score in public.

Show Notes

Michael Johns, business transformation consultant at Lydonia Technologies, joins Chris Hutchins to work through where agentic AI lands first in a health system: the back office. They cover which revenue cycle, claims and finance tasks are ready for autonomous agents, what should never be handed over, and how least privileged access and audit trails keep agent decisions defensible. Michael closes with what separates successful programs: treating automation as a program, telling people what is in it for them, and keeping score in public.

What We Cover

  • Why agentic AI is landing in the healthcare back office before the bedside
  • Claims processing, denials management, prior authorization and other high-volume manual work
  • Returns moving from robotic process automation to agentic workflows, and the 80/20 rule for knowledge workers
  • An aging population, a shrinking labor force, and why AI will have to take on work
  • Swivel work between the EHR and financial applications
  • What never goes to an autonomous agent: clinical judgment, patient safety, ethical decisions and empathy
  • Least privileged access, audit trails and defensibility for agents
  • Tenancy issues, browser extensions, embedded SaaS AI and shadow AI
  • How agent deployments quietly fail, and why smoke and stress testing matter
  • Centers of automation and treating agentic automation as a program
  • Psychological safety, what is in it for employees, and keeping score publicly

Key Takeaways

  • Start where the volume and repetition are. Per Johns, the best first targets are high-volume, highly repetitive manual work in finance and accounting, revenue cycle and claims. The closer work gets to the patient, the more judgment and experience matter, which makes the bedside the last place he would start.
  • Agents need the same boundaries as people. Least privileged access applies to bots and agents just as it does to humans: define which applications they use and which data they can and cannot see, and keep an audit trail that proves what an agent touched. He adds that agents do not give up and work around the clock, so missing backstops let damage happen fast.
  • The return comes from what people do with the time. Johns describes returns of two to three times investment with robotic process automation and four to seven times with agentic workflows. He argues the larger impact comes from moving skilled staff from remedial work to strategic work, which requires leaders to explain what is in it for employees, make it safe to bring ideas forward, and celebrate wins publicly.

Examples Used in the Conversation

  • Chris's early billing operations work, including a stack of commercial claims about two and a half feet tall
  • An agent with HR system access that has no need to see Social Security numbers or salaries
  • Office 365 tenancy crossing between organizational and personal accounts while an agent screen scrapes a website
  • Chris's experience of an AI tool pivoting into adjacent data after hitting a barrier, without telling him
  • An invoice address written as "Fifth" one time and "5th" the next, throwing data extraction off
  • A revenue cycle goal of taking DSO from forty days to thirty-eight, then celebrating the people who got it there
  • George Hodson's lesson to Michael early in his career: what gets rewarded gets done

These examples reflect Michael Johns' perspective and experience and are offered as leadership insight. They are not legal, clinical or compliance advice for any specific organization.

About Michael Johns

Michael Johns is a business transformation consultant at Lydonia Technologies, where he works with large organizations as they adopt automation and agentic AI. He brings three decades of experience across enterprise IT, operations, sales leadership and business transformation, including roles at Xerox, EMC and Pomeroy. He is also a board member and an Amazon international best-selling author, and has called Louisville home for more than twenty years. Connect with him on LinkedIn.
